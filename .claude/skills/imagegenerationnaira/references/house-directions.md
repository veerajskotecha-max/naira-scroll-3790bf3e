# House directions

What the brand has liked, what it scored, and what the palette actually is. Take a direction from
here before inventing one. The full record of each shoot lives in `scripts/higgsfield/campaign-*.md`.

anti-brief. Do not propose it.

## What makes a frame score

- **One idea per frame, and it can be described in a sentence.** An impossible event, a material
  doing something it should not, a broken physical law, a measurement proved against the body. Two
  ideas in one frame reads as neither.
- **Festive through material, never iconography.** Colour, lacquer, glass, silk — not diyas, not
  rangoli, not symbols.
- **Copy space is part of the composition, not a margin.** Two-fifths of every frame is left clear,
  unbroken, and free of hard shadow, because these go out as Instagram posts and paid ads with type
  and layers added on top. A beautiful frame with no room for type is a failed frame.
- **The pale palette is cream, bone, blush, pale lilac.** The colour directions are saturated blocks
  against it, not gradients.

## Light

Two sources, always:

- one **large soft window frontal-left** for the skin and the ground,
- one **small hard specular kicker right** for the metal.

Without the second source the model renders gold as matte plastic and clear stones as grey glass.
This is the single most reliable quality lever in the whole pipeline — name both sources in every
prompt.

## Casting a campaign

Six SKUs for a standard campaign; **nine to twelve when the brief is to cover the
catalogue** — that is the size at which a direction stays coherent and still
earns its own palette and divider page. Change direction every nine to twelve
frames, not every six.

Six to twelve SKUs. Spread the categories — six frames should not be four rings. Prefer `ACTIVE` over `DRAFT`
and SKUs with four or more live images. Exclude anything already used in a previous campaign unless
you are deliberately re-shooting it. Choose on looks, from a visual pick sheet of the unused SKUs,
never from the names.
