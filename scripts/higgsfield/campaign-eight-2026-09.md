# Eight campaigns — September 2026

Fifty-eight frames, one per SKU. **Average 9.23.** Per campaign:
COLOUR THEORY 9.08 · SLOW BURN 8.83 · THE PALE HOURS 8.92 · SOFT PHYSICS 9.17 · HOW IT'S WORN 9.17 · SOFT MATTER 9.45 · CLEAR 9.42 · LILAC HOUR 9.48.

**Coverage is complete.** There are 71 jewellery SKUs on Shopify (90 products
minus 19 with an empty `productType`, which are clothing). 55 of the 71 are
ACTIVE, and every one of those 55 now has a campaign frame. The 16 DRAFT SKUs
are deliberately excluded — a beautiful frame of an unbuyable SKU is worth
nothing as an ad. Three DRAFT SKUs were shot in the earlier campaigns and are
flagged in the book.

Nothing was uploaded to Shopify or posted. The frames live outside this repo;
the standing proof sheet carries them beside the live product images they were
checked against.

## Method

Nano Banana Pro, 4:5, 2K, one product per frame, two to three references each —
the SKU's own live product-page images, live before supplier, flattest view
first. Every frame was re-checked against **all** of that page's images and
against `sizing.md`. 26 of the 58 frames were re-shot; the September three
campaigns held a ship bar of 9.5.

Five supplier photographs were cross-checked against their live pages and
**three were rejected** as the wrong product or a multi-product sheet: the Pearl
Reverie Bracelet's supplier shot carries green stones the live piece does not
have, the Blush Station Bracelet's is a colourway sheet, and the Rivière of
Light Bracelet's is a stack of unrelated bracelets.

## What these three campaigns taught

**1 · I was wrong about the product three times, and the model was right.**
The brief said the Petal Pearl Drop Studs had four plain petals; the live macro
shows five ribbed petals, a pavé halo and stones scattered across them. It said
the Blush Station Bracelet's stones were cabochons; they are faceted. It said
the Clover Charm carried five stones; it carries four and a gold bead. In each
case the model followed the photograph over my sentence. **Zoom to full
resolution before writing the fidelity clause, not after the frame comes back.**

**2 · The model inverts relief.** Asked twice for a groove cut into wax, it
returned a raised welt bearing the same impression. Asked to put the chain
*inside* the trench — a geometry rather than a direction — it complied at once.
When a physical effect fails twice, restate it as where things are, not as what
happened.

**3 · Some conceits are not renderable and should be replaced, not retried.**
Two passes at a bracelet half-sunk in opaque cream produced no cream at all. The
same idea in chalk powder — a medium the model already handles — worked first
time. Swapping the medium is cheaper than a third pass.

**4 · Overcorrection is still the second failure of every re-shoot.** The Pearl
Reverie Bracelet's centre ring went from too small to correct-but-square. v3 was
kept over v4 because a ratio miss is smaller than a shape error.

**5 · Contact-sheet size lies in both directions.** Campaign VIII's shadow-swap
frame looked like a failure at montage size and was perfect at full resolution.
Judge nothing from a thumbnail.

**6 · Name the absence, not the intent.** "The shadows go up" produced downward
shadows. "No shadow below or beside either earring, none pooling at the base"
produced upward ones.
