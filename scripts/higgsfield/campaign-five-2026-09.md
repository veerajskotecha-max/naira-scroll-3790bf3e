# Five campaigns — September 2026

Thirty frames, one per SKU, in the three directions the brand keeps coming back
to plus two built from them. **Average 9.03.** Per campaign:
COLOUR THEORY 9.08 · SLOW BURN 8.83 · THE PALE HOURS 8.92 · SOFT PHYSICS 9.17 · HOW IT'S WORN 9.17.

Nothing was uploaded to Shopify or posted. The frames themselves live outside
this repo; the standing proof sheet carries them beside the live product images
they were checked against.

## Method

Nano Banana Pro, 4:5, 2K, one product per frame, two to three references each —
the SKU's own live product-page images, live before supplier, flattest view
first. Every frame was then re-checked against **all** of that page's images,
not only the ones it was shot from, and against `sizing.md`.

Each frame is built with a deliberate empty field so type and layers can be set
on top, and so it crops to 1:1 and 9:16 without losing the piece.

13 of the 30 frames were wrong first time and were re-shot. Every one was
caught by pixel-ratio normalisation — scaling the frame and the live photograph
so one shared feature is the same number of pixels — not by looking at a contact
sheet.

## What carried over

**1 · The two-light rule.** One large soft window frontal-left plus one small
hard specular kicker right. Named in every prompt. Without the second source the
model renders gold as matte plastic and clear stones as grey glass. It is the
single sentence that separates photographed from generated.

**2 · Pixel-ratio normalisation is the only verification that works.** Scale the
frame and the live photo so one shared feature — a bead, a link, a stone, an ear
— is the same pixel width, then compare. Every miss in these thirty frames was
invisible at contact-sheet size and obvious once the ruler matched.

**3 · Where the angles differ, measure something internal.** A foreshortened ear
or a tilted hoop compresses one axis and inflates every ratio taken against it.
Stone count around a hoop, beads along a bar, links between two beads — internal
ratios survive rotation. The body is the ruler only when both pictures show it
from roughly the same angle.

**4 · Overcorrection is the second failure of every re-shoot.** R5's heart went
from 1.57 beads to 3.04 before landing at 2. W3's ring went from 0.80 of a
finger to 0.44 before landing at 0.57. State the target as a ratio with a
number, never as a direction.

**5 · Check what the reference actually is.** W3's second pass drifted because
the slot meant for the SKU's worn photograph held a second packshot, and W5's
held a macro on green velvet. Neither frame had a body reference at all. Open
every reference before uploading it.

**6 · A campaign about scale is the one the comments ask for.** HOW IT'S WORN
scored 9.17, tied highest with SOFT PHYSICS, and it is the only set that answers
"how big is it really" — the question under every jewellery post.
