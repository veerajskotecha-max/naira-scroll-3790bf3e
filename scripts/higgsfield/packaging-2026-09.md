# Packaging — September 2026

Thirty-five frames built from the real Naira box, shot 15 September 2026.
Fifteen of the box alone, twenty of one SKU each seated on the drawer's black bed.

Model `nano_banana_pro` (reports as `nano_banana_2`), 4:5, 2K, two-fifths of every
composition left clear for type. 80 credits spent across 43 submissions (35 keepers,
8 re-shoots). Nothing uploaded to Shopify, nothing committed but this record.

## The box, as briefed

A square slide-drawer box: a blush outer sleeve and a drawer that slides out sideways.
The face is a true square 70mm by 70mm and only 25mm deep — a shallow tile, never a cube.
The sleeve is pale blush-rose paper with a fine suede-like nap and a faint pearlescent
sheen. The drawer is lined with matte black flocked velvet; **its black bed is 62mm
across**, and that number is the ruler every product frame is measured against.

## The mark, and the failure it caused

The box carries **four letters — N, A, R, A — with a drawn rose standing in the gap
between the A and the R**. Getting that right was the whole risk of this shoot.

The first brief listed the characters as *"N, A, a ROSE, R, A"*. Two frames out of
fifteen took that literally and typeset the word: `NA(rose)RA` and `NA[ROSE]RA`.

The fix, which worked in one pass both times, was to stop calling the rose a character:

> FOUR LETTERS ONLY — N, A, R, A. Between the second letter A and the third letter R is
> a gap one letter wide, and standing in that gap is a SMALL DRAWN FLOWER — a picture,
> not a letter and not a word. No brackets, no parentheses, no dividers around it.
> The English word 'rose' does not appear on the box in any form.

A third frame drew the right idea as the wrong flower — an open bloom with splayed
petals. Naming the silhouette as geometry fixed that too: *closed, tight, unopened, one
smooth compact teardrop pointed at the top, taller than it is wide — the height of a
capital, the width of a third of one — on one straight unbroken stem to the baseline,
two narrow leaves low down.* Plus the absence list: no daisy, no spread petals, no round
flat head, no flower wider than it is tall.

Re-shot, and why:

- **P4 Star Point Band** — came back a flat wide slab carrying three oversized solid
  stars. Restating it as geometry fixed it: *a plain closed circular band, two rims
  parallel circles, metal 1.5mm thick, a clear open hole two-thirds of the diameter
  through the middle*, with each star *2mm — one third of the band's height, small
  enough that three could stack within the band's width*.
- **P5 Blush Cluster Ring** — the mark drew an open bloom (see above).
- **P12 Prism Rivière Bracelet** — v1 came back colourless. Naming the sequence brought
  pink and aqua but dropped the yellow. v3 fixed it by counting: *of twenty-one stones,
  seven pink, seven aqua, seven lemon, in strict rotation, so a yellow falls every third
  link*, plus *no colourless, white or cream big stone anywhere in the line*.
- **P14 Blush Station Bracelet** — **my brief was wrong.** I asked for clear runs of bare
  chain between the stones; the live page shows the stones set close, in two alternating
  sizes, with an extender and a ball. The photograph won, as it always does.
- **P18 Baroque Pearl Lariat** — the oval came back smooth and the pearl came back round.
  Fixed by counting the flutes (*about sixteen grooves across the visible face, ridge,
  valley, ridge, valley, so the hard light breaks into a row of separate bright lines*)
  and by naming what a baroque pearl is not (*not a sphere, not a smooth teardrop —
  wider on one side, two or three soft dents, no axis of symmetry*).
- **P19 Clover Charm Necklace** — gave a cable chain where the live page shows a bead
  chain. Fixed by describing the chain as *a row of tiny round gold balls strung one
  against the next, each catching its own pinpoint highlight*, and by listing what it is
  not: no oval links, no rolo, no flat snake, no smooth wire.
